# Flavor: Entrepreneur

Read this on top of `pipeline.md` when the task is evaluating a business idea, market,
pricing, go-to-market, or any venture decision. It *adds* to the base stages — it does not
replace them. Default to FULL rigor for any major resource commitment.

## FRAME — identify the riskiest assumption first
- What is the **riskiest assumption** — the single thing that, if false, kills this idea?
  Name it first.
- Who is the customer, specifically? Not a demographic — a person with a specific painful
  problem.
- What painful problem are you solving, and how does the customer cope today without you?
- What would have to be true (market, customer, model, team) for this to work?
- What is the **kill criterion** — the signal that would tell you to stop?

Add to the FRAME artifact: `Riskiest assumption`, `Customer (specific)`, `Problem & current
coping`, `What would have to be true`, `Kill criterion`.

## RESEARCH — ground it in real signals, not optimism
- **Real demand signals:** search trends, communities, Reddit threads, job postings,
  existing spend. Find them, or explicitly note their absence.
- **Competitors and alternatives**, including the "do nothing" option and substitutes.
  What's been tried and failed, and why?
- **Market size, bottom-up** from customer segments × count × willingness-to-pay — not a
  top-down TAM slide.
- **How customers cope today** — often the most revealing signal; it's the switching cost
  you're fighting.

Add to the RESEARCH artifact: `Demand signals (with sources or "absent")`, `Competitors &
alternatives`, `Market size (bottom-up)`, `How customers cope today`.

## PLAN — sequence by riskiest assumption, define unit economics
- **Riskiest assumption first:** sequence the *cheapest test that could invalidate it*
  before building anything. Don't build the product before testing demand.
- **Unit economics (rough but required):** CAC, price/ARPU, gross margin, payback period.
- **Kill criteria up front** so you can't move the goalposts later.
- **Distribution:** how do the first 100 customers find this? "Build it and they will come"
  is not a plan.

Add to the PLAN artifact: `Riskiest assumption to test first`, `Cheapest invalidating test`,
`Unit economics`, `Kill criteria`, `Distribution plan (first 100)`.

## DRAFT — note
Produce the actual analysis / recommendation / model / GTM plan — not a list of things to
think about. No "it depends" without saying what it depends on and why that matters.

## CRITIQUE — run all of these
- **Survivorship bias:** pattern-matching to winners while ignoring the many who tried this
  and failed?
- **Confirmation bias:** did research only surface supporting evidence? What would a skeptic
  search for?
- **Distribution reality:** a specific, plausible path to the first 100, or is "word of
  mouth" doing the heavy lifting?
- **Why now / why you:** what changed to make this viable now? Why this team?
- **Regulatory / operational landmines:** licensing, legal, infra, partnership dependencies?
- **Unit-economics stress test:** do the economics hold if CAC is 2× or price is 30% lower?

Add to the CRITIQUE artifact: `Bias checks`, `Distribution reality`, `Why now / why you`,
`Landmines`, `Unit-economics stress test`.

## VERIFY — tie claims to evidence, not optimism
- **Demand signals are real:** each demand claim tied to a specific signal (trend, thread,
  conversation, sale, sign-up). "People will want this" backed by nothing is not verified.
- **Market size is bottom-up:** verify the math (segments × count × WTP).
- **Competitor picture is current:** if web search is available, confirm competitors still
  operate, with the features and pricing stated.
- **Kill criteria are specific:** each is actually observable and measurable.

## DELIVER — lead with a recommendation
- **Recommendation:** PURSUE / TEST-FURTHER / KILL — one word, then the reason. Not "it depends."
- **Evidence driving it.**
- **Next cheapest experiment** to validate or kill the riskiest assumption (what, cost,
  timeline, what pass/fail looks like).
- **What would change the recommendation.**

## Extra tripwires
- **Goalpost moving** — kill criteria vague enough to always pass → make each specific and measurable.
- **Optimism laundering** — framing all evidence charitably while ignoring bearish signals
  → run the survivorship and confirmation-bias checks explicitly.
